**The basis:** Dethrone the Evil leader and his lackeys, fight people in trivia matches, win their question sets and money. Each answer set only has so many questions. Once you run out, you can recycle them, but they'll only do 1/2 damage. You answer a question from your bank to attack, and answer a question from your opponent’s bank to defend. If you get their health low enough, you get a special format question as their last attempt to beat you. You have 5 chances, each one takes a 1/4 of the HP you had before the Last Chance. After a successful fight, you win a question set, and money! You can flee (the button is called “run away!”) and you don’t get anything. If you run out of HP, you get a game over screen, and you have to return to a checkpoint. I keep saying “HP” but I don’t think, in canon, a character dies. You just get ashamed and run away. For NPCs, this means giving up your stuff.

**Genre touchstones and 2D/3D:** Gameplay like pokemon, visually, more like an eldrich combination of Procession to Calvary or Paper Mario. In other words, some scenes are in 2.5D or 2D with depth that mirrors 3D (I really wanna avoid having to learn blender if I can help it. I know Photoshop though) Whereas some streets or interiors of buildings are completely 2D. 

**Aesthetic:** It’s all completely made out of public domain images, and images I’ve taken, collage style. I want the world to look like it’s taped and thumbtacked together. During level design, I’ll add a bunch of tape, tacks, paperclips, etc. 

**Bars:** To progress in the game, you fight in Bar Trivia at special locations known as Bars. It's basically Pokemon gyms. To progress to a new area, you have to beat the earlier Trivia Hosts and earn their Bar Badger (not a typo, it's a badger with a hat that denotes which world it came from)

**Quests:** Sometimes, to get someone to battle you, you need to complete a quest for them. As An example, a bird scientist might want you to find all three new species of birds

**Magic Magnifying glass:** You can investigate potential battlers to get hints about what categories they have, what they're weak against, and/or what special types they have, all using the magic magnifying glass. This can also be used to find out what items are interactable. (Everything turns black and white and muted except for interactable objects, which turn yellow and glow)

**Economy:** There’s money too. After each battle, you win money that you can spend in shops on items, like hints, health potions, and mana potions. You can also spend them at armories and inns.

**Armories:** Another Mechanic is the Armories. You can fine-tune your Answer Sets, upgrade their strength, and swap sub-categories. Costs coins

**Inns:** You rest and restore all of your mana and health for a fee.

**Mana and spells:** During battle, you can cast spells that cost mana. This might heal you, it might auto-damage enemies, it might double the gold the enemy drops, it might give a hint.

**Twitch:** Twitch integration so you can have chatters choose answers. There may also be a chaos mode where chat does everything. It wouldn’t be as much “Go left, go right, hit X” as there’d be a command over an interactable object, and chat would go, for example, “!character” to interact with the character named “Character” (There will be no characters named character)

**Play modes (single-player and stream):** The game must be fully playable by one person with no stream, no chat, and no second player. Every choice that chat might make has a local input path (mouse/keyboard) that any player can use on their own. Twitch voting is an optional overlay layered on top when streaming: the same underlying "answer round" mechanic (question + options + a source of picks) drives both, so local input and chat votes feed one shared tally rather than forking the game logic. Solo play is the baseline; streaming is additive.

**Character customization:** Every player character is made of three parts, plus a hat that you can unlock in stores or find around the world. Those are the head, the torso/arms, and the legs. You earn the ability to copy the parts of each character you beat in battle. Hats probably have perks, but you can beat the game without ever finding or buying a hat. But why would you? Hats are fun!

**Core progression**  
No traditional XP or numerical levels.  
The game has 7 worlds, each with a thematic Bar that functions as the world's major progression challenge.  
Defeating the Bar earns a Bar Badger (working name), which unlocks the next world and increases your maximum HP. Maximum mana increases whenever maximum HP increases (so Bars are what grow your mana pool too). Defeating a Bar also fully heals you — HP and mana — but gaining question sets/ammo never does.  
Each Badger can have a unique costume/hat matching its world.  
Questions = ammunition  
Each trainer has a question set, generally around:  
12 for easy trainers  
16 for normal trainers  
20 for tough trainers  
24–32 for bosses
Set size reflects how tough the trainer is. Difficulty is a property of the *trainer*, not of individual questions: each trainer is an expert in their field and their set spans easy-to-hard on that topic. Per-question difficulty labels are not a mechanic. Special-format questions come at the very end of a trainer's set.
Defeating a trainer unlocks their entire question set for the player.
Questions are consumed when used in battle.  
Fresh question: full damage.  
Previously seen question: half damage.  
Questions you've unlocked are permanently part of your collection even after you've used them.  
Your maximum HP is based on your overall knowledge/progression, not how many questions you currently have left.  
Combat  
You answer a question from your collection to attack.  
You answer a question from the opponent's collection to defend.  
Trainers have HP and increasingly difficult questions as the worlds progress.  
When a trainer reaches roughly 20% HP, they get a special last-chance question with a different format.  
Shops can eventually sell additional question sets and combat items, but we're leaving the economy for later.  
Postgame  
After defeating the final boss, you've finished the story but still have lots of unused questions.  
Technopolis eventually gets a battle simulator where you can replay defeated trainers specifically to burn through your remaining questions.  
The ultimate completion goal is to use every question in the game.  
Once you've exhausted the entire question library, a final peaceful-world cutscene plays and you get the true/final credits.  
Afterward, you can still wander around and talk to everyone, but you've officially beaten the whole game.

The core philosophy:

Questions are your ammunition. Bar Badgers represent permanent progression. HP represents how much you've learned. Coins are for the economy.

**Story**  
Every year, the secret society, The King Tut Oriel summons a random person from the real world, writes up a prophecy, sends them to dethrone the evil king. Nobody gets very far. This year it's you, a college student who is very much under prepared.
